How a mend is made

There is no timer and no rush. The whole round is the path of one finger, the knots in the way, and whether the thread is still in one piece at the end.

Pick the stitch for this tear

Each patch shows its own tear and a pattern of points. Read that pattern before the stroke starts. The layout changes from patch to patch.

Drag through every point

Guide the needle in one continuous stroke. Lifting your finger ends the attempt. The thread only counts if it visits the points in a single pass.

Keep the tension bar low

Curves leave the thread loose. Sharp corners push the bar up. The goal is to finish under the target, not to race the clock.

Steer around the knots

Knots add tension when the thread crosses them. A clean route goes through the points and leaves those knots alone.

Earn a Clean Stitch

Land the stroke under the tension target and the tear closes. Hit 100% and the thread snaps. That spool is gone.

Cloth

A tear of its own

Twenty handcrafted patches, each with a different tear and point layout. Warm linen, wood, and thread art set the table.

Spools

A snap spends thread

Every patch gives you three spools. A snapped thread is not a hint you can ignore. When the spools run out, that mend is over.

Hands

One finger, portrait

Controls are built for portrait play. You hold the device and draw the stitch. Nothing else has to be tapped mid-stroke.

Skip

An ad, if you are stuck

The listing says a short ad can skip a patch you cannot close. The download is free, and the store marks the game as containing advertising.

Steady hands, not a stopwatch

Move smoothly and the thread stays loose. Yank it around a corner and tension climbs until it snaps. The better solve is the quieter line: every point, no knots, still under the mark.
